# Crypto Guide for Beginners

How would you introduce blockchain and crypto to a specific person with no prior knowledge? Which question comes first, what example would you use and how would you tell whether your explanation worked?

## Everyone contributes

Use the first 20 minutes to write your own [learning path](../templates/learning-path.md). Choose five to seven steps and write one of them in the words you would actually say. Submit it individually before discussing it with the group. Describe your reader without identifying them.

## The guide team's brief

Turn the drafts into a book outline that takes a newcomer from their first question to a practical exercise. LIGHT UP has more than 700 scripted videos to draw on later. Work from the participants’ drafts first, then look for existing material to support the chapters.

1. Divide the drafts among team members. Give each draft an anonymous reference and tag its opening question, key concepts, examples and exercises.
2. Compare the proposed sequences. Identify a prerequisite that several writers agree on and a point where reasonable authors would teach things in a different order.
3. Write an annotated table of contents. For every chapter, name the reader’s question, the learning objective, an example or instruction and a way to check understanding.
4. Develop two plausible openings and one sample two-page spread. Write the spread as it would appear in the book, including an example and a small exercise a reader can attempt.
5. Ask a peer to read the spread, do the exercise and mark the first unexplained term. Revise one passage based on what you observe. Record what still needs testing with actual beginners.

## What to hand in

Use the [guide team template](../templates/guide-team.md). Keep the original individual drafts alongside the synthesis in the agreed collection channel so the editors can revisit minority viewpoints and distinctive examples. Submit a public summary only within the reuse terms agreed with contributors. Open the pitch by reading an opening aloud or asking the audience to try the sample exercise.
