# LIGHT UP × TUM Blockchain Club Hackathon

How would you choose a crypto exchange, a software wallet or a tax tool if you could see exactly how every rating was earned?

On Saturday, 28 November 2026, 20–25 students form ten teams of two or three. Three tracks each take on a whole LIGHT UP comparison: what to rate, how to rate it and how to test it. People test what only people can judge, such as how easy an app is to use. Agents collect and check the public facts. Every track ends with something LIGHT UP can use after the event. Several teams work on the same track independently, so LIGHT UP receives competing proposals. Andi and Peed from LIGHT UP introduce the tracks with real examples and support the teams.

## Before the event

- The TUM Blockchain Club shares set-up documents and tutorials for the tech stacks you can build with.
- Install the AI tools the club recommends (Gemini, KiroCrew) before you arrive.
- Read the three track briefs and download the rating matrices.
- If you want to open test accounts in the exchange track, bring your smartphone and an ID document. For the optional cost test you need €10 of your own money and a way to deposit it. Taking part without an account is fine.

## Choose your track

| Track brief | Guiding question | What LIGHT UP gets out of it |
|---|---|---|
| [Crypto Exchange Comparison](tracks/01-exchange.md) | How can we improve the crypto exchange comparison as a whole? | Revised criteria for the categories you choose, a usability test of five exchanges with real sign-ups and timings, a €10 cost test, and agents that collect public facts |
| [Crypto Wallet Comparison](tracks/02-wallet.md) | What would a good software wallet comparison look like if we built it from scratch? | A provider list, criteria, scoring rules and weights for a new comparison, tried on three wallets |
| [Crypto Taxtool Comparison](tracks/03-taxtool.md) | How can we improve the crypto tax tool comparison as a whole, including how it rates prices? | Revised criteria, a test with a shared sample dataset and the basis for a cost calculator |

Each track takes three or four teams. At team formation, the teams of one track agree on different focus areas, for example one category each, so that together they cover the whole comparison. The vote judges the result, not the size of the focus.

Each brief shows the matrix it starts from, taken from the published methodology. Andi and Peed cross-check each other's findings and use community feedback when reviewing weights, so a proposal from this hackathon goes to a community poll before it changes the matrix. Every exchange in the comparison is tested with €1,000 of real money, from deposit through a bitcoin purchase to withdrawal to a personal wallet. [Read the methodology and download the rating matrices (German).](https://lightupkryptos.de/so-testen-wir/)

## The brief for every team

Start from the matrix in your brief. Define each scoring rule before you look at any product, then try it on two or three products. Show the current and proposed approach side by side. Ask a team from another track to reproduce one result using only your instructions. Record how long the check took and what would trigger the next one. A spreadsheet, sketch, short script, prototype or worked example is welcome. The directions in each brief are suggestions; choose your own if you can demonstrate it during the hackathon.

A proposal can add or remove a criterion or a category, change its scoring or points, or change how the result appears to readers. If you change points, show how the rest of the matrix is adjusted so that the total stays at 100. [Submission template](templates/comparison.md)

## Working with agents

People test, agents collect. Use your agents for public facts: fee pages, licence registers, documentation, app store data, audits. Your own impressions from hands-on tests go into the agent as a prompt you write yourself, using the [hands-on test log](templates/hands-on-test.md).

Agents never get access to your accounts. Do not give them logins, passwords, verification codes, ID documents, API keys, seed phrases or screenshots that show personal data. Blur personal data before you share any screenshot with your team.

## Submission and voting

Use the [submission template](templates/comparison.md). One team member submits the approved public material in a pull request under `submissions/<track>/<team>/`. Teams without a GitHub account can hand the same files to the organizers.

Each participant casts one vote for a team other than their own. Consider three questions equally. Does the result help the intended reader? Does the example support the claim? Can LIGHT UP use or test it after the event? The team with the most valid votes wins. In a tie, hold a runoff between the tied teams. The club may prepare a testnet ballot with one voting credential per participant and a check against voting for one's own team. A simple form is the fallback.

## Editorial ground rules

- Cite sources, record the test date and distinguish observations, estimates and missing evidence. Keep the weaknesses visible even for the highest-scoring product.
- Use plain language. For German website copy, follow LIGHT UP house style, including "Du" and "Sicherheit".
- Keep the work educational. Exclude price predictions, trading signals, individual coin recommendations, investment advice and paid placements.
- Use public sources, your own hands-on observations, approved extracts or clearly labelled synthetic data. Keep personal information, account data, raw audience records, affiliate revenue, partner contracts and licensing terms out of submissions.

## Organizer checklist

- **LIGHT UP:** Prepare the track introductions with one real example each, provide dated matrix extracts and be available to the teams during team work. Explain the affiliate links in the exchange track at the start.
- **TUM Blockchain Club:** Share set-up documents and tech-stack tutorials, ask participants to install the AI tools beforehand and prepare the vote.
- **Together:** Confirm venue, spoken language and prize. Keep prize sponsorship independent of providers in the comparisons. Before collecting work, tell participants which materials will be public and agree how code, data and writing may be reused and credited.
- **After the event:** LIGHT UP reruns promising tests, takes proposed weighting changes to its community and records which changes are adopted and why.
