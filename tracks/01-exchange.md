# Crypto Exchange Comparison

**Guiding question:** How can we improve the crypto exchange comparison as a whole?

## Starting point

LIGHT UP compares 59 exchanges. The matrix has eight categories and 100 points:

| Category | Criteria today | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Security | Registered office 10 · year founded 6 · BaFin or MiCA licence 7 · Trustpilot 4 | 27 |
| Costs and transparency | Deposit and trading costs in the €1,000 test 10 · withdrawal 2 · transparency 8 | 20 |
| Self-custody | Can customers withdraw crypto to their own wallet? | 18 |
| Usability | User-friendliness 5 (interface, app and desktop, German) · features 5 (order types, performance tracking, card, interest on fiat) · payment methods 7 (PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay, credit card, EUR, German IBAN, standing order) | 17 |
| Savings plan | Does the exchange offer one? | 7 |
| Customer support | Response time, quality and language | 5 |
| Offer | Number of coins and tokens 4 · stablecoins 1 | 5 |
| Staking | Does the exchange offer staking? | 1 |

Every criterion with its scoring rule: [full exchange matrix](../matrices/exchange.md).

Some criteria rest on judgement. The user interface is rated "clear", "acceptable" or "not clear", support "good", "medium" or "poor", transparency "very transparent", "transparent" or "not transparent". Others describe the company only from the outside: security checks where an exchange sits and whether it is licensed, not how it protects the assets it holds.

## Hands-on test

Everyone who wants to opens an account at two or three of the five exchanges. Choose so that each exchange is tested by several people. Opening an account costs nothing; money is only needed for the optional cost test below.

Follow the same steps at every exchange and note the results in the [hands-on test log](../templates/hands-on-test.md):

1. **Sign-up.** Start a timer when you click the link. Stop it when the account is created and your e-mail or phone number is confirmed.
2. **Fee page.** Find the page with the current trading fees. Note the time and where you found it.
3. **Bitcoin purchase.** Prepare a purchase of €10 in bitcoin up to the confirmation screen. Note what the screen shows before you confirm: price, fee, total. If you have no balance, note whether you get that far at all.
4. **Withdrawal.** Find where you would withdraw bitcoin to your own wallet.
5. **Language and platforms.** Switch the app to German. Check whether there is an app and a desktop version.
6. **Overall impression.** Rate the user interface on the matrix scale: clear, acceptable or not clear. Note where you got stuck.

### Cost test with €10 (optional)

Do the costs an exchange shows match the costs you actually pay? To find out, deposit €10 and buy bitcoin for it.

1. Before you confirm the purchase, note the price, fee and total shown, and the time.
2. In the same minute, note the bitcoin price in euros from a public reference source. Agree on one source for the whole track before you start.
3. After the purchase, take from the transaction history what you paid in euros, including any deposit fee, and how much bitcoin you received.
4. Actual cost = euros paid minus the value of the bitcoin received at the reference price. Compare it with the fee shown in step 1. Your agent can do the calculation from the numbers you type in.

Check beforehand that €10 is above the exchange's minimum deposit and order size. Note the deposit method and whether you used the simple or the advanced trading view, because fees can differ. The €10 test does not replace LIGHT UP's own test with €1,000 per exchange: at small amounts, fixed and minimum fees weigh more. It shows whether the displayed costs are accurate. The money is yours, and taking part is voluntary.

### Privacy and taking part

Taking part is voluntary. If you do not want to upload an ID, or your verification is rejected, test as far as you get without one, or pair up with someone. Your agent never sees your account: you write your observations into the prompt yourself. Blur personal data on screenshots. You can close the account after the event in the exchange's account settings or through its support.

## Your task

Each team takes one focus. Pick it at team formation; each focus goes to one team only. Teams A and B open the accounts; share your hands-on test logs with the whole track. Every team also answers the [weights question](../README.md#weights-question-for-every-team) for its categories.

| Team | Focus | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange A | Usability | Run the hands-on test at two or three exchanges with timings. Propose a usability rating that two testers would score the same way, covering user-friendliness, features and payment methods. |
| Exchange B | Costs and transparency | Run the €10 cost test and compare fee page, order preview and actual cost. Propose what "very transparent" means and how the spread enters the rating. |
| Exchange C | Security | Look beyond company facts: proof of reserves, account protection such as security keys and withdrawal address whitelists, past incidents and whether customers were compensated, what a BaFin or MiCA licence actually requires. Propose new security criteria. |
| Exchange D | Data agent | Build an agent that keeps the public facts of the comparison current: for the five exchanges, it collects licence register entries (BaFin, ESMA), fee pages, the number of coins, payment methods, savings plan, staking and German language, each with source and date, and flags what has changed since the last run. Show which criteria it can fill on its own and which still need a person. |

Define every scoring rule before you look at any exchange. Then apply it to two or three of the five.

**Build option:** A data agent as above, or a page that shows a reader how an exchange's points add up, with the hands-on results next to the matrix.

## What LIGHT UP gets out of it

Revised criteria for usability, costs and security, each with scoring rules and points. Usability results for five exchanges from several testers each, with timings, and a check of displayed against actual costs. Agents that keep the public facts current. What LIGHT UP considers sound goes into the next version of the matrix, after the usual community poll.

## What to submit

Complete the [comparison submission](../templates/comparison.md):

2. Results for two or three exchanges, with sources and dates.
3. The hands-on test logs of your team, without personal data.
4. One sentence per new criterion that explains to a beginner why it matters.
5. The outcome of a peer's attempt to reproduce one result using only your instructions.
6. How long one check took, and what would trigger the next one.
