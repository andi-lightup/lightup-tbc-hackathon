# LIGHT UP × TUM Blockchain Club Hackathon

How would you choose a crypto exchange if you could see exactly how every score was earned? And how would you explain crypto to a friend who had never heard of a blockchain?

This proposed three-hour workshop brings 30–40 students together to tackle both questions. Everyone writes a short learning path for a beginner. Participants then work in five teams. Four teams improve a LIGHT UP comparison, while the fifth turns the individual learning paths into the outline of a book. Andi and Peed from LIGHT UP support the teams.

The figures above describe the published methodology used to prepare this workshop. Andi and Peed cross-check each other’s findings and use community feedback when reviewing weights. One existing test follows €1,000 from deposit through a bitcoin purchase to withdrawal to a personal wallet. The question for this workshop is where another hands-on test would earn its upkeep, and where a simpler check would serve readers just as well. [Read the methodology and download the comparison matrices (German).](https://lightupkryptos.de/so-testen-wir/)

## The brief for comparison teams

Pick a decision a reader struggles with. Find the relevant row in the matrix and improve one thing about it. You can add or remove a criterion, change its scoring or weight, redesign how the evidence appears, or build a small tool that keeps the data current. The ideas in each brief are examples. Choose your own if you can demonstrate it during the workshop.

Try the change on two or three products. Show the old and proposed approach side by side. Ask someone from another team to reproduce one result using only your instructions. Record how long the check took and what would trigger the next one. A spreadsheet, sketch, short script or worked example is welcome.

Consider the editorial value of the data. What question could a journalist answer by citing a LIGHT UP table, chart or dataset? Draft the question and show what evidence a full study would require. A workshop sample establishes whether the method works. Market-wide claims require broader coverage. [Comparison submission template](templates/comparison.md)

For a two-hour session, collect individual learning paths and track preferences in advance. A compact agenda allows 10 minutes for the introduction, 55 for team work, 15 for peer review, 30 for pitches and 10 for voting.

## Submission and voting

Use the template linked from your track brief. One team member submits the approved public material in a pull request under `submissions/<track>/`. Teams without a GitHub account can hand the same files to the organizers. Keep individual drafts and unpublished book passages in the collection channel agreed with contributors. A public summary can link to the track brief and describe what was tested.

Each participant casts one vote for another team. Consider three questions equally. Does the result help the intended reader? Does the example support the claim? Can LIGHT UP use or test it after the event? The team with the most valid votes wins. In a tie, hold a runoff between the tied teams. The club may prepare a testnet ballot with one voting credential per participant and a check against voting for one’s own team. A simple form is the fallback.

## Editorial ground rules

- Cite sources, record the test date and distinguish observations, estimates and missing evidence. Keep the weaknesses visible even for the highest-scoring product.
- Use plain language. For German website copy, follow LIGHT UP house style, including “Du” and “Sicherheit”.
- Keep the work educational. Exclude price predictions, trading signals, individual coin recommendations, investment advice and paid placements.
- Use public sources, approved extracts or clearly labelled synthetic fixtures. Keep personal information, raw audience records, affiliate revenue, partner contracts and licensing terms out of submissions.

## Organizer checklist

- Andi and Peed refine the briefs, then review the proposal with the [TUM Blockchain Club](https://www.tum-blockchain.com/). Confirm date, venue, duration, spoken language, team preferences and prize. Keep prize sponsorship independent of providers in the comparisons.
- Prepare dated matrix extracts and small test packs for the selected examples. Each brief lists its inputs. Agree who will distribute the packs and collect book drafts.
- Before collecting work, tell contributors which materials will be public and agree how code, data and writing may be reused, credited or included in the book.
- After the event, rerun promising tests, take proposed weighting changes to the LIGHT UP community and try competing book openings with readers who are new to crypto. Record which changes are adopted and why.
