# Crypto Exchange Comparison

**Guiding question:** How can we make the security rating of crypto exchanges say more about how safe a customer's money actually is?

## Starting point

LIGHT UP compares 59 exchanges in eight categories. Security is the heaviest category at 27 of 100 points. Today it is built from four criteria:

| Criterion | Scoring | Max. points |
|---|---|---|
| Registered office | DACH, Luxembourg, Liechtenstein: full points · Europe: 8 · USA: 6 · other reputable jurisdictions (e.g. Singapore, Dubai, Israel, Japan): 3 · offshore jurisdictions (e.g. Seychelles, Cayman Islands, British Virgin Islands): 0 | 10 |
| Year founded | Before 2016: full points · after 2024: 0 · in between: pro rata | 6 |
| BaFin or MiCA licence | Yes: full points · no: 0 | 7 |
| Trustpilot | Points based on rating and number of reviews, full weight from 500 reviews | 4 |

All four describe the company from the outside: where it sits, how long it has existed, whether it is supervised and what customers say. None of them checks how the exchange protects the assets it holds. In a period of AI-assisted attacks and frequent hacks, that is the gap this track should close. [Full methodology and matrix (German)](https://lightupkryptos.de/so-testen-wir/)

## Your task

Propose a better security rating. Keep the 27 points as the frame, unless you can argue for a different weight. You decide which criteria stay, which go and which are added. Possible directions:

- **Proof of reserves:** Does the exchange publish one, how often, and does it cover liabilities as well as assets?
- **Account protection:** Hardware security keys, withdrawal address whitelists, withdrawal delays after a password change.
- **Track record:** Past security incidents and whether customers were compensated.
- **Licence depth:** What a BaFin or MiCA licence actually requires, and whether both deserve the same points.
- **Asset segregation and insurance:** What the exchange states about cold storage and coverage, and how much of it can be verified.

For each criterion, define the scoring rule before you look at any exchange. Then apply it to two or three exchanges, using only public sources.

## What LIGHT UP gets out of it

A proposal LIGHT UP can put into its matrix: a revised list of security criteria, each with its scoring rule and maximum points, tested on two or three exchanges. What LIGHT UP considers sound goes into the next version of the matrix, after the usual community poll, so that the comparison becomes more transparent and easier to follow.

## What to submit

Complete the [comparison submission](../templates/comparison.md):

1. The current and proposed security criteria side by side.
2. Results for two or three exchanges, with sources and dates.
3. One sentence per new criterion that explains to a beginner why it matters.
4. The outcome of a peer's attempt to reproduce one result using only your instructions.
5. How long one check took, and what would trigger the next one.
